Justification for the Excel figure:

> What software did you use to create your data visualization?

Microsoft Excel

> Who is your intended audience? 
    
My intended audience was myself, I was curious to see what the change in consumer price index looked like across different categories of food. It's hard to tell exactly with a stacked graph, but overall, all food categories appear to be increasing at similar rates. But this figure could be useful to anyone curious about increasing food prices.

> What information or message are you trying to convey with your visualization? 
    
I was trying to show whether any particular food items/categories had sharper price increases. That doesn't really seem to be the case. Maybe pork is more expensive recently?

> What aspects of design did you consider when making your visualization? How did you apply them? With what elements of your plots? 
    
I think it's hard to show this many curves in colour, but the Excel defaults seemed fine. It's hard to tell which category is which exactly but I think that's fine because at a glance, there overall trend of the CPI is clear (it's increasing) and that the increase in individual categories is pretty similar. It would be better to show a scatter/line plot of the CPIs of the individual categories.

> How did you ensure that your data visualizations are reproducible? If the tool you used to make your data visualization is not reproducible, how will this impact your data visualization? 
    
There's no random sampling involved so if the data doesn't change, the figure should be reproducible. The original data was a table in an excel file so I just copied the cells where the data were located. If the data get updated, probably the latest year's data, just extend the data range by one column.

> How did you ensure that your data visualization is accessible?  
    
I changed the fonts to be larger, darker (higher contrast) compared to Excel defaults.

> Who are the individuals and communities who might be impacted by your visualization?  
    
Not sure ... me personally this data doesn't really change my buying habits. For this to be useful to other people, I think maybe you need to include some stat about yearly wage/salary/income increases. But even then, average statistics probably won't apply to particular individuals. In terms of food, it really depends their particular situation.

> How did you choose which features of your chosen dataset to include or exclude from your visualization? 
    
I chose to plot the CPI dataset because I thought it would be interesting. Within the dataset, I plotted all available data.

> What ‘underwater labour’ contributed to your final data visualization product?

On the Ontario gov website you can see the names of employees which updated this dataset. There index itself also needed to be estimated, basket defined, etc. from a team at Statistics Canada. The fact that this data was in a nice Excel spreadsheet made plotting pretty easy on my end.
